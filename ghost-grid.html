The Ghost Grid

A little constellation of hand-built sensors that listen to the invisible wireless world around one house — and report everything back to one place that speaks out loud.

Built by Zach  ·  a maker's project

In plain English

The air around us is full of invisible signals all the time — the WiFi our phones use, little bursts of radio from gadgets and remotes, the quiet chatter of the modern world. Most of it we never notice.

I've been building small devices that can hear that world. On their own they're neat little toys. The fun part was getting a whole pile of them to work together — like a tiny radar station for the airwaves — and to quietly flag when something looks out of place. Then I taught the whole thing to talk, so it can just tell me what it sees.

What one night looked like

This went from a messy pile of half-working parts to a real, running system — in a single sitting:

  • 01Untangled a tangle of code down to one clean copy, and fixed two stubborn bugs that were blocking everything.
  • 02Hand-flashed a dozen little gadgets and got each one awake and doing its job.
  • 03Got two radios talking to each other — and taught one to relay across a mile.
  • 04Wired every sensor into one hub, so nothing it hears gets lost.
  • 05Best part: taught the whole grid to speak — it announced itself out loud at 3 a.m. (quietly, so the house could sleep).
